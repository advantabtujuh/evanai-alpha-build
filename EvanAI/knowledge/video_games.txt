A video game is an electronic, interactive game played via a user interface—such as a controller, keyboard, or motion sensor—that provides visual feedback on a display, like a TV, monitor, or phone screen. These games, which often include audio and haptic feedback, range from simple puzzles to complex, immersive, and social, virtual worlds. 

Key aspects of video games include

Interaction, Users actively control elements (characters, cameras, or objects) on the screen rather than passively watching.
Platforms, They can be played on consoles (PlayStation, Xbox), personal computers, mobile devices, and VR headsets.
Elements, They often involve rules, goals, and challenges, sometimes incorporating immersive storytelling, professional audio, and high-fidelity graphics.
Genres, These are diverse, including action-adventure, RPG (Role-Playing Game), strategy, simulations, and sports, among others. 

Video games have evolved from simple 1950s prototypes into a massive, multibillion-dollar industry that is a dominant form of modern entertainment.
