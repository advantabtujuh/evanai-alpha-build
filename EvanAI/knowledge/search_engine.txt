A search engine is a software program or online tool designed to help users find information on the internet by searching for keywords or phrases. These systems crawl, index, and rank billions of webpages using complex algorithms to return the most relevant results, such as websites, images, or videos, in response to user queries. 

How Search Engines Work

Search engines follow three primary steps to provide results
Crawling Bots, (spiders) scan the internet to discover content, navigating through links on websites.
Indexing The discovered content is stored and organized in a massive database, allowing for quick retrieval.
Ranking or Serving Results When a user searches, the engine uses algorithms to rank the stored pages by relevance, displaying the best results first. 

Key Components and Functions

User Query, The keywords or phrases a user types into the search bar.
Algorithm, A set of rules that decides which pages are most relevant and how they are ordered.
SEO (Search Engine Optimization) Techniques used to make websites more visible to search engines. 

Popular Examples

Google, The most popular search engine, holding the largest share of global searches.
Bing, Developed by Microsoft.
Yandex, Widely used in Russia and other regions.
DuckDuckGo, Focuses on user privacy, not tracking searches.
Yahoo! or Baidu, Other prominent search tools. 

Search engines are essential for navigating the internet efficiently and can also operate on databases, in app stores, or on social media platforms.
